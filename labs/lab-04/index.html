Lab 04 · Week 4 · September 25, 2026

Recover the Hidden Message

Build a tuned RLC receiver, measure which frequencies it favors, and use it to recover a hidden Morse-code word from competing signals

Learning Outcomes

By the End of This Lab

  • Build and verify a series-RLC band-pass filter
  • Predict its resonant frequency from component values
  • Measure input and resistor-output voltage over a controlled frequency sweep
  • Use Vout / Vin to identify the measured pass-frequency peak
  • Recover a tone-coded message from stronger out-of-band signals
  • Explain one capability and one limitation of receiver filtering

The Big Picture

What You Are Doing

  1. Calculate the expected resonant frequency of the assigned RLC circuit.
  2. Build the circuit and measure its response at ten frequencies scaled to your team's predicted resonant frequency while keeping the source settings fixed.
  3. Use frequencies from `0.05 f0` to `20 f0` to observe how the capacitor and inductor shape the full band-pass response without building separate RC and RL circuits.
  4. Graph measured `Vout / Vin` against normalized frequency `f / f0` and identify the frequency favored by the circuit.
  5. Replace the function generator with a prerecorded composite signal and use the same circuit to recover a hidden Morse-code word.

Before Lab

Preparation

  • Review inductive reactance, capacitive reactance, impedance, series resonance, and band-pass filters
  • Bring a calculator and open the Lab 4 report before beginning
  • Remember that the function generator's High-Z display setting does not remove its internal output resistance; measure the actual circuit input with the oscilloscope
  • Connect every source ground, oscilloscope ground, and speaker-input ground to circuit common
  • Never connect the computer output and function-generator output at the same time

Bench Setup

Equipment and Materials

  • Two-channel oscilloscope with two 10x probes
  • Function generator with BNC-to-red/black clip lead
  • Solderless breadboard and jumper leads
  • One 10 Ω resistor
  • One 2.9 mH inductor
  • One nonpolar 1.00 µF capacitor
  • Calculator and Morse-code reference
  • Shared message recovery station

Follow These Steps

Lab Walkthrough

Part 1: Prepare the Measurement Bench

  1. Open the Lab 4 report and enter your name and lab partners.
  2. Use the analog lab's LCR meter to get the measured resistance, capacitance and inductance of each component. Do this by pressing "LCR Auto".
  3. The inductor has a winding resistance. Take note of that in the LCR meter.
  4. Calculate the predicted resonant frequency using f0 = 1 / (2 pi sqrt(LC)).
  5. Set both physical probes and oscilloscope channels to 10x and DC coupling. Connect both probe ground clips only to circuit common.
  6. Set the function generator to a 2 Vpp sine wave, 0 V offset, and High-Z display mode, but keep its output disabled.

Build the Series-RLC Filter

Evidence Photo

You will take one evidence photo in this section. Keep the capacitor, inductor, resistor, source connection, common node, and both probe points visible.

  1. With the source disabled, build one series path from generator signal through C, then L, then to OUT. Connect the 10 Ω resistor from OUT to common.
  2. Connect Channel 1 from circuit IN to common to measure the actual Vin. Connect Channel 2 from OUT to common to measure Vout across the resistor.
  3. Double check the build with the circuit diagram before enabling the source.
  4. Photograph the verified circuit and probe locations.

Measure the Frequency Response

Evidence Photo

You will preserve one oscilloscope capture at the measured peak. Keep both channels, frequency, amplitudes, scales, and probe factors readable.

  1. Use your team's predicted f0 from Table 1. For each multiplier in Table 2, calculate target frequency = multiplier x f0. Do not use another team's frequency targets.
  2. Sweep these normalized frequencies in order: 0.05 f0, 0.20 f0, 0.50 f0, 0.75 f0, 1.00 f0, 1.25 f0, 2.00 f0, 5.00 f0, 10.0 f0, and 20.0 f0.
  3. At each frequency, record target frequency, measured frequency, measured Vin, measured Vout, and H = Vout / Vin. Use measured Vin, not the generator display, because source resistance changes the voltage delivered to the circuit.
  4. Make an XY scatter plot of H = Vout / Vin against f / f0. Use a logarithmic horizontal axis from 0.05 to 20 and a linear vertical axis. Plot your measured values, not the nominal generator values.
  5. Identify the frequency with the largest measured Vout / Vin. It may not occur exactly at 1.00 f0 because of component tolerance, winding resistance, source resistance, and breadboard or probe loading.
  6. Save the peak-response capture before changing connections.
  7. Compare the measured peak with the predicted resonance and use the graph to decide whether the sweep shows a band-pass response.

Part 2: Check the Unfiltered Message

  1. Disable and disconnect the function generator before attaching the computer source.
  2. At the shared station, first connect the computer directly to the powered speaker with an AUX cable.
  3. Play the assigned composite track once. Record what you hear and why the intended word is difficult to distinguish.
  4. Stop playback.

Recover the Hidden Message

Evidence Photo

You will preserve the message-station input and filtered output in this section.

  1. Connect the BNC LEFT OUT clip lead to circuit IN and common. Red goes to IN; black goes to common.
  2. Connect the second clip lead from circuit OUT and common to BNC LEFT IN. Red goes to OUT; black goes to common.
  3. Keep Channel 1 on filter IN and Channel 2 on filter OUT. The oscilloscope and speaker now observe the same filtered output in parallel.
  4. Replay the same composite track at the approved volume. Use the provided Morse reference to decode the repeated word.
  5. If the message is not clearer, stop and ask the instructor to check the peak frequency, common connections, source level, and speaker loading before changing components.

Finish and Submit

  1. Complete all three Content tables, including component values, calculations, ten-point normalized response data, the response graph, and the message comparison.
  2. Complete the Evidence section with the circuit photograph, peak-response capture, and message-recovery capture.
  3. Complete the Conclusion in your own words and answer every listed question.
  4. Save the completed report, export it as a PDF, and submit the PDF through Learning Suite.

Why This Lab Matters

  • Receiver filtering can preserve availability by favoring an intended channel while reducing out-of-band interference
  • A selective filter cannot remove interference that overlaps its passband
  • Input-versus-output measurements support claims about receiver behavior but do not identify the source or intent of interference
  • This activity uses a wired prerecorded signal and does not radiate or jam an external communication system
