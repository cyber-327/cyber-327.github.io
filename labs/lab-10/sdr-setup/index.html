Lab 10

WebSDR Signal Guide

Find three separate transmissions: ordinary AM (DSB with a carrier), USB and LSB.

Back to Lab 10

Start WebSDR

  1. WebSDR — start here. Open Northern Utah WebSDR. Find three separate intelligible transmissions: ordinary AM (DSB with a carrier), USB and LSB. Ordinary AM supplies the DSB example; it is not a fourth required signal.
  2. Choose a receiver covering the band you want to explore. Click START to enable browser audio, then increase the volume gradually. Check mute and your computer’s audio output if you hear nothing.
  3. Find the frequency-entry box and check its units. In a kHz field, enter 1160 for 1160 kHz, or 14290 for 14.290 MHz. The spectrum shows signal level across frequency; the waterfall shows how signals change over time. Zoom in enough to separate nearby signals.
  4. Record the receiver’s name, URL and geographic location from its page. This is the location of the remote antenna and receiver. The transmitter can be somewhere else; you do not need to locate it.

Find ordinary AM: DSB with a carrier

  1. AM / DSB — choose the broadcast band. On the Utah landing page, select WebSDR1 ("Yellow") → AM Broadcast (1160 kHz).
  2. Select AM and keep the default AM filter initially. Look for a strong, narrow carrier peak in the spectrum, or a steady carrier line in the waterfall. Center the tuning on that carrier.
  3. Listen for understandable speech or recognizable music. If the starting channel is unclear, explore nearby broadcast channels until you find an intelligible signal.
  4. Zoom in around the carrier while speech or music is present. Look for signal energy on both its lower-frequency and higher-frequency sides. The carrier and both sidebands support identifying ordinary AM as DSB with a carrier.
  5. Save a screenshot showing the carrier, both sidebands where visible, frequency, AM setting and spectrum/waterfall. Record your audio and spectrum observations in the AM / DSB row of Table 2.

Find an upper-sideband signal: USB

  1. USB — choose the 20-meter band. Return to the landing page and select WebSDR2 ("Green") → 20 meters. Select USB.
  2. Explore approximately 14.150–14.350 MHz. Look for narrow patches of activity that change while someone speaks. Amateur conversations can have gaps between transmissions; choose an active voice signal.
  3. Tune slowly across the signal. If the voice sounds unusually high or low, make smaller frequency adjustments until speech sounds natural. Use the receiver’s default USB filter initially.
  4. When correctly tuned, look for speech energy mainly above the tuned reference frequency. A strong continuous carrier is normally absent. The reference marks where the suppressed carrier would be, so do not require a visible carrier line.
  5. Save a screenshot while the speaker is transmitting, with frequency, USB setting and spectrum/waterfall visible. Record what you heard and the observations supporting USB in Table 2.

Find a lower-sideband signal: LSB

  1. LSB — choose the 40-meter band. Return to the landing page and select WebSDR1 ("Yellow") → 40 meters. Select LSB.
  2. Explore approximately 7.175–7.300 MHz. Look for active voice transmissions and tune in small steps until speech sounds natural. Use the receiver’s default LSB filter initially.
  3. When correctly tuned, look for speech energy mainly below the tuned reference frequency, usually without a strong continuous carrier. Compare its position with the frequency scale.
  4. Save a screenshot during speech, with frequency, LSB setting and spectrum/waterfall visible. Record your audio and spectrum observations in the LSB row of Table 2.

Check and record your findings

  1. Use the spectrum and audio together. The selected mode button alone does not identify the transmission. An ordinary AM broadcast can also be heard using USB or LSB because it contains both sidebands. Find separate single-sideband transmissions rather than submitting the same AM broadcast three times with different receiver settings.
  2. If a band is quiet or a signal is too weak to identify, choose another receiver covering that band from the Utah landing page or WebSDR directory. Record the receiver you actually use. Fading or a weak carrier alone does not prove that a signal is single sideband.
  3. For each of the three signals, record frequency with units, receiver name/location/URL, selected mode, an intelligible-audio observation and the spectrum evidence supporting the type. Caption all three screenshots with those settings and observation time, then close the receiver tabs when finished.
