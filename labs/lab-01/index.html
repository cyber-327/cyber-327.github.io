Lab 01 · Week 1 · September 4, 2026

The Series Circuit

Ohm's Law, resistor tolerance, series current, voltage dividers, power, and prediction-versus-measurement evidence

Scheduled duration: 2 hours after Friday lecture

Learning Outcomes

By the End of This Lab

  • Measure resistance, voltage, and current with a DMM using the correct connection method
  • Predict and verify current and voltage drops in a low-voltage series circuit
  • Evaluate whether measured components and circuit results are consistent with tolerance and instrument uncertainty
  • Locate an altered component, hidden resistance, reference-path fault, or source mismatch from node-voltage evidence
  • Write a claim-evidence-reasoning conclusion that distinguishes direct observations from supported inference

The Big Picture

What You Are Doing

  1. Build a known three-resistor series circuit and verify that it behaves as predicted
  2. Use the measured values from that circuit as a trusted baseline or electrical fingerprint
  3. Visit two instructor-prepared fault stations built from the same intended circuit, but with one concealed change
  4. Compare voltages point by point, locate the first segment that differs from the baseline, and support a diagnosis with evidence

Before Lab

Preparation

  • Review the Week 1 definitions, formulas, resistor color code, and series-circuit diagram
  • Bring a calculator and open the Lab 1 report before beginning
  • Do not energize or rewire anything until directed by the walkthrough and the in-lab safety demonstration

Bench Setup

Assigned Equipment and Data

  • Digital Multimeter (DMM)
  • Current-limited DC power supply with red and black leads
  • Solderless breadboard and jumper leads
  • R1 = 330 ohm, R2 = 1.0 kilohm, and R3 = 3.3 kilohm resistors
  • Instructor-prepared fault stations X and Y

Follow These Steps

Lab Walkthrough

  1. Inventory your station: Find the DMM, power supply, breadboard, leads, jumper wires, and all three resistors; record your name and lab partners in the report
  2. Learn the breadboard connections: Use the markings or continuity demonstration provided in class to identify which holes form one node; make sure the two resistor leads are placed in different connected regions rather than shorted together in one node
  3. Check the DMM leads: Put the black lead in the COM jack and the red lead in the voltage/resistance jack; do not use a current jack until the current-measurement step
  4. Configure the power supply: Keep the output disabled while connecting the red lead to positive and the black lead to negative; use the instructor-demonstrated controls to set 5.00 V and a 25 mA current limit, then leave the output disabled
  5. Read the resistor markings: Use the color-code table to identify the rated resistance and tolerance of R1, R2, and R3; enter these values in Table 1
  6. Measure resistance with power off: Select resistance mode, touch one probe to each end of R1, and record the measured value; repeat for R2 and R3, calculate percent difference, and decide whether each resistor is within tolerance
  7. Predict the circuit: Before applying power, calculate the total resistance, voltage across each resistor, node A and B voltages, series current, and total power; enter the predictions in Table 2
  8. Build the series circuit: Connect supply positive to R1, R1 to node A, R2 to node B, R3 to node G, and node G to supply negative as shown in the diagram; have another person trace the complete path before enabling the output
  9. Measure voltage in parallel: Confirm black is in COM, red is in the voltage/resistance jack, and the DMM is in DC-voltage mode; enable the supply, place the probes across each resistor, and record the three voltage drops in Table 2
  10. Check voltage polarity and nodes: Reverse the probes across R1 and record what changes; then keep the black probe on node G and use the red probe to measure nodes A and B; record the results and compare node voltages with resistor drops
  11. Measure current in series: Disable the supply before moving leads, move the red DMM lead to the appropriate current jack, and select DC current; open the circuit after R3 and insert the meter into that gap, then enable the supply and record the current in Table 2
  12. Verify series current: Disable the supply before each change and repeat the current measurement between R1 and R2 and then between R2 and R3; compare the readings, disable the supply, restore the circuit, and immediately return the red DMM lead to the voltage/resistance jack
  13. Establish the baseline: Finish Table 2, compare predicted and measured values, calculate percent differences, and take the required setup and measurement photographs for the Evidence section
  14. Investigate fault station X: Do not open, rewire, or resistance-test the station; measure its source, input, node A, node B, and node G voltages, compare them with the baseline, complete Table 3, and take the required photograph
  15. Investigate fault station Y: Repeat the same point-by-point voltage comparison, complete Table 4, and take the required photograph
  16. Finish the report and reset the bench: Complete the Evidence and Conclusion sections, disable and zero the supply, return the DMM leads to COM and the voltage/resistance jack, and return all components as directed

Why This Lab Matters

  • In industry, technicians commonly troubleshoot failed or altered hardware by comparing node voltages and signal levels with a known-good baseline
  • Point-by-point measurements help locate the circuit segment where expected behavior first changes
  • The same evidence-first method supports hardware maintenance, communications troubleshooting, and investigation of possible physical tampering
