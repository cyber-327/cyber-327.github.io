Lab 09 · Week 9 · October 30, 2026

Intercept the Link CTF

Observe a communication link, recover its message through a clean channel, and restore communication through an impaired channel

Learning Outcomes

By the End of This Lab

  • Use two scope inputs to distinguish I/Q symbol states from voltage-versus-time traces
  • Determine symbol timing and translate an ordered symbol sequence into message bits
  • Recover a four-character command from measured I/Q states
  • Compare sampled bit errors and mapped bit rate while changing one allowed control
  • Separate reliable delivery from confidentiality, authentication and freshness

The Assignment

Your Mission

  1. Observe the signal states and timing on the oscilloscope.
  2. Recover a four-character message through the clean connection, then measure its error performance.
  3. Restore communication through the impaired connection by selecting a rate that meets both delivery requirements.

Before You Connect

Preparation

  • Review I/Q, symbol rate, mapped bit rate, sampling margin, intersymbol interference and BER.
  • Open the Lab 9 report. Use separate serial-monitor windows for the labeled TX and RX USB ports, both at 115200 baud, 8-N-1, with Newline selected. The TX console controls the allowed transmitted rates; RX controls its sampling and decisions.
  • Open the DC-09 service schematic and the separate I/Q workbench. Use HELP on each console. Do not replace the firmware or read source files to obtain flags.
  • The receiver reports decoded data and known-pattern error counts. The scope displays voltages; it does not calculate BER for this activity.

Bench Setup

Equipment and Materials

  • Keysight InfiniiVision DSOX2012A oscilloscope and two compensated 10x probes
  • Two prepared classic 5 V Arduino Nanos, labeled TX and RX, with USB data cables
  • Breadboard, jumpers and the I/Q channel components listed in the service schematic; students build the circuit
  • Computer with two available USB ports (or a suitable hub), Arduino IDE serial consoles, calculator and the individual report

Optional or Specialized

  • prepared RX display-blanking lead to the scope's rear EXT TRIG input

Case File

Your DC-08 assessment exposed weaknesses in the door receiver. The security team now asks you to investigate the DC-09 connection between an access panel and its door controller.

The vendor claims its unfamiliar signaling format keeps service traffic private. Use the diagnostic connector and recovered service schematic to investigate the message.

During a maintenance update, a replacement line filter disrupts communication. Restore reliable delivery with the new filter still connected.

The Investigation

Investigate the Link

1. Reconnaissance — Table 1

Evidence Photo

Save your completed wiring, time capture establishing symbol timing, and X–Y state groups, with device IDs and scope settings.

  1. Open the DC-09 service schematic. Identify the boards labeled TX (transmitter) and RX (receiver). Keep their preloaded sketches; do not upload new firmware.
  2. Gather two 330 Ω resistors, two 1.5 kΩ resistors, one 2.2 kΩ resistor, one 10 kΩ resistor, two 100 nF capacitors and jumper wires. Set the 33 µF capacitors aside for Part 3.
  3. Unplug both USB cables. Choose a breadboard GND rail and connect both boards' GND pins to it. Join split rail sections if needed. Leave the two 5 V pins separate and leave AREF unconnected.
  4. Choose an empty connected breadboard row and label it I_RX. All connections named I_RX below must meet on this same row.
  5. Connect TX D9 through a 330 Ω resistor to I_RX.
  6. Connect I_RX by a jumper to RX A0.
  7. Connect a 1.5 kΩ resistor from I_RX to the GND rail.
  8. Connect one 100 nF capacitor from I_RX to GND. This is the clean REF connection; do not add the 33 µF capacitor yet.
  9. Choose a different connected row and label it Q_RX.
  10. Connect TX D10 through the second 330 Ω resistor to Q_RX.
  11. Connect Q_RX by a jumper to RX A1.
  12. Connect the second 1.5 kΩ resistor from Q_RX to GND.
  13. Connect the second 100 nF capacitor from Q_RX to GND.
  14. Choose a third row and label it SYNC_RX. Connect TX D2 through the 2.2 kΩ resistor to this row.
  15. Connect SYNC_RX to RX D2. Connect the 10 kΩ resistor from SYNC_RX to GND. No capacitor goes on SYNC.
  16. Before applying power, check every connection against the service schematic. Confirm the resistor values, common ground, separate signal rows and two 100 nF capacitors. Ask for help if the wiring does not match.
  17. Connect RX's USB cable first, then TX's. Open two Arduino IDE windows and select the matching port for each board. Open Serial Monitor in each window at 115200 baud with Newline selected. The serial format is 8-N-1. Label the windows TX and RX; send HELP to each to check the connection.
  18. Set both scope probes and both channel probe factors to 10×. Use DC coupling. Attach both probe ground clips to common GND, CH1's tip to I_RX and CH2's tip to Q_RX. Start near 1 V/div and adjust to show both levels.
  19. Send HOLD 00 to TX. Record the measured high voltage for I and Q. Measure RX's actual supply at its 5 V pin relative to GND using a meter or the scope.
  20. Send HOLD 11 to TX and record both low voltages. Then send HOLD 01 and HOLD 10, one at a time, and check them against the service-sheet state table. Confirm both signals stay within RX's measured supply range.
  21. Send STOP to TX. Put the scope in Normal time mode and move CH1's tip to SYNC_RX. Send RATE 200 and then START to TX. Measure the interval between adjacent rising SYNC edges; do not use the repeating period of I or Q data as the symbol interval.
  22. Send STOP to TX, move CH1 back to I_RX, and send START. Keep CH2 on Q_RX. Press Horiz → Time Mode → XY to view the combined states. Adjust scales and positions so you can see all state groups; connecting paths are normal transitions.
  23. Save the wiring, symbol-timing and X–Y evidence. Record the measured levels and state count in Table 1, then calculate symbol rate, bits per symbol and mapped bit rate. Check your setup before continuing: both consoles respond, all four held states match the schematic, and you can see timing pulses and state groups.

2. Intercept the Message — Table 2

Evidence Photo

Save the ordered 16-symbol time capture, your observed states and decoded four-byte command, and the clean-reference receiver counts.

  1. Keep both 100 nF REF capacitors connected. Send STOP, MODE INTERCEPT and RATE 200 to TX, one command per line. TX must remain stopped while you prepare the scope.
  2. The X–Y display shows available states but does not preserve message order. Return the scope to Normal time mode. Press the channel 1 and channel 2 buttons so both channels are on. Connect CH1 to I_RX, CH2 to Q_RX, and both probe ground clips to common GND. Use DC coupling and matching 10× probe settings.
  3. Start near 1 V/div on each channel and position the traces so their low and high levels are visible. Expect low near 0 V and high near your measured high voltage, approximately 4 V. You can separate overlapping traces vertically; use each channel's own 0 V reference when reading its voltage.
  4. Set the horizontal scale to 10 ms/div. Press Trigger and select trigger type Edge, source 2 (CH2), and slope Rising.
  5. Turn the physical Trigger Level knob on the front panel. Watch the trigger voltage in the upper-right corner of the display and set it halfway between your measured Q low and high voltages, approximately 2 V. This tells the scope to capture when CH2 rises through that voltage; it does not change the signal's voltage.
  6. Press Horiz → Time Ref → Left, then use the horizontal position knob to set the horizontal delay to 0 s. The trigger triangle at the top of the grid should be near the left edge. This marks the start of the message and leaves room to see the rest to its right.
  7. Check that at least eight horizontal grid divisions are visible to the right of the trigger marker. At 10 ms/div, those eight divisions show the full 80 ms message. Each 5 ms symbol occupies half a division, so two symbols fit in each division.
  8. Press Single on the scope. Only after the scope is waiting for a trigger, send CAPTURE to TX. TX sends the four-byte maintenance message once and then stops. If the capture misses the start, arm Single again and repeat CAPTURE.
  9. Before CAPTURE, flat traces at 0 V are normal. After CAPTURE, expect two digital traces switching between their low and high levels over 80 ms, then returning low. The scope should freeze this record. Keep the capture on screen while you read it.
  10. Find the first CH2 rising edge at the trigger marker. Treat this as time 0, the beginning of symbol 1. Ignore the idle levels before the message and the stopped levels after its 16 symbols.
  11. Read both channels at the same instant, near the middle of each 5 ms symbol. Use a time cursor: read symbol 1 at 2.5 ms after the trigger, symbol 2 at 7.5 ms, symbol 3 at 12.5 ms and symbol 4 at 17.5 ms. Continue adding 5 ms for each symbol until symbol 16 at 77.5 ms. At 10 ms/div, the first reading is a quarter division after the trigger, followed by half-division steps.
  12. At each reading, record CH1 as I High or Low and CH2 as Q High or Low. Use the level in the middle of the interval rather than the changing edge. Repeated symbols can hold the same voltage for several intervals: count time intervals, not pulses or transitions.
  13. Each I/Q pair is one symbol carrying two bits. Translate the simultaneous levels using the service-sheet map: I High / Q High → 00; I Low / Q High → 01; I Low / Q Low → 11; I High / Q Low → 10. A trace's screen position is not a bit value; use that channel's measured low and high voltages.
  14. Open the I/Q workbench. Enter symbol 1's observed I/Q pair in slot 1, then continue left to right in time order through slot 16. Read the two channels together for each slot; do not enter all of CH1 and then all of CH2.
  15. Every four symbols produce eight bits, which form one byte and one character. Use the workbench to obtain the bit pairs, four hex bytes and decoded four-character command, and record them in Table 2. Save the original time capture and the workbench output; a guessed word alone is not evidence.
  16. Prepare RX for the clean-reference measurement. Enter VREF followed by RX's measured supply in millivolts. Calculate each signal's midpoint as (measured low + measured high) / 2, convert to millivolts, and enter THRESH followed by the I midpoint and Q midpoint. The service page gives exact syntax.
  17. Send RATE 200, PHASE 50 and STATUS to RX, one line at a time. Check that STATUS shows your chosen values and ready=1. Opening a serial port can reset a board; re-enter settings if this happens.
  18. Send START to TX to restore the framed stream. CAPTURE alone does not send training frames. Wait for RX to announce INTERCEPT and confirm a valid received message; it will report the length and CRC status without revealing the text.
  19. Send RUN to RX and wait for the next complete 1,024-bit training comparison. Record wrong bits, compared bits, BER and any frame or timing failures. No compared bits means BER is unavailable, not zero. If the stage changes during a RUN, start a fresh RUN after the announcement.
  20. Keep your 16-symbol capture, decoded command and clean-reference count record in Table 2. Check before continuing: you have decoded four characters from measured states and obtained a complete baseline measurement.

3. Restore the Link — Table 3

Evidence Photo

Save the initial failed condition, the selected restored condition, both confirming count records, and the recovered flag with the challenge network still connected.

  1. The replacement line filter has disrupted service. Your task is to restore delivery while keeping that filter connected. Save the failed condition as well as the condition that works.
  2. Before disconnecting anything, send STATUS to RX and save its settings. Keep your measured RX supply and the I and Q midpoint thresholds from the clean connection; you will enter them again after power is restored.
  3. Send STOP to TX. Unplug TX's USB cable, then unplug RX's. Keep the common ground wiring in place.
  4. Remove the 100 nF capacitor from I_RX to GND. Replace it with one 33 µF electrolytic capacitor: positive lead to I_RX, negative lead to GND. The negative lead is usually marked with a stripe; check the part's marking.
  5. Replace the Q_RX capacitor in the same way: one 33 µF capacitor, positive to Q_RX and negative to GND. Use one selected capacitor per signal; do not leave the 100 nF capacitors in parallel.
  6. Keep both 330 Ω series resistors, both 1.5 kΩ resistors and the SYNC divider in place. Check capacitor polarity and connections against the service schematic before powering up. Label this connection CHALLENGE.
  7. Reconnect RX's USB first, then TX's. Open the matching serial consoles at 115200 baud with Newline selected. Send HELP to each if needed. Power removal resets settings.
  8. On RX, send VREF followed by your measured supply in millivolts, then THRESH followed by your saved I and Q midpoint values in millivolts. For example, the syntax is VREF v and THRESH i q; replace the letters with your measurements.
  9. On RX, send RATE 200, PHASE 50 and STATUS, one command per line. Check the restored values and ready=1. On TX, send MODE RECOVERY, RATE 200 and START, one command per line. Use START for this part; CAPTURE contains no training frames.
  10. Return the scope to Normal time mode, with CH1 on I_RX and CH2 on Q_RX. Keep DC coupling and matching 10× factors. Resume continuous acquisition if it is still stopped after Single; use Auto triggering if needed. Adjust the time scale to show several symbols. Save the rounded pulse shapes at 200 symbols/s and compare them with your clean reference. You may also compare the X–Y state groups.
  11. Send RUN to RX. Save the result at 200 symbols/s, including wrong bits, compared bits, BER and any failure messages. A failing connection may never announce RECOVERY. Do not wait for that announcement before testing another rate. RUN times out after 90 seconds if it cannot complete a comparison; record NO LOCK or unavailable BER rather than zero errors.
  12. Keep the challenge network connected, and keep VREF, thresholds and PHASE 50 fixed throughout the rate comparison. Change only the symbol rate. If you change another setting while troubleshooting, repeat the affected comparisons with the restored settings.
  13. Test 50 symbols/s: send RATE 50 to TX and RATE 50 to RX. Send STATUS to RX to confirm its settings, then RUN for a fresh comparison. Save the waveform and count result. If RX announces a stage change during RUN, start a fresh RUN after the announcement.
  14. Test 25 symbols/s the same way: RATE 25 on both consoles, then RUN on RX. Record all three candidates in Table 3, including failures. Calculate mapped bit rate as two bits per symbol times the symbol rate.
  15. Choose the fastest tested rate that meets both requirements: mapped bit rate of at least 80 bit/s and observed BER no greater than 0.01, using 1,024 aligned known-pattern payload bits per run. NO LOCK fails. A slow, error-free candidate can still fail the rate requirement. If no candidate passes, check the wiring and saved settings before continuing.
  16. Set your selected rate on both TX and RX. Wait for RX to announce RECOVERY; a complete cycle can take about 35 seconds at the slowest rate. If a candidate cannot decode the stage, it is not a passing configuration. Keep the selected rate, phase, thresholds, VREF and channel unchanged for confirmation.
  17. Send RUN to RX and save a COMPLETE result with 1,024 compared bits and no more than 10 wrong bits. After that result, send RUN again and save a second complete qualifying result. These must be two fresh RUN measurements; one record copied twice does not count. Changing settings, a failed run or a timing fault clears the qualifying-run count.
  18. With two qualifying runs and a valid received recovery message, RX prints Recovery confirmed: followed by the flag. Copy the full received flag into Table 3. It arrives through the link; you do not type a flag or a door command into RX to unlock it.
  19. Save the initial failed condition, one rejected candidate, your chosen configuration, both confirming count records, before/after waveforms and the recovered flag. Identify one change in the waveforms that supports your explanation. A flag alone does not replace the measured evidence.

4. Finish and Submit

  1. Complete Tables 1–3, the three evidence groups and the short conclusion. Each student submits an individual PDF report.
  2. Stop transmission before moving probes or channel jumpers. Restore the posted station state, close Serial Monitor, and disconnect USB as directed.

Why This Matters

  • Physical access can reveal unencrypted traffic even when its waveform is unfamiliar
  • A degraded channel can prevent legitimate messages from arriving correctly or on time
  • A matching error check and reliable reception do not establish an authorized or fresh message
  • The scope and error counts support a link diagnosis; neither establishes attacker identity or intent

Submission
