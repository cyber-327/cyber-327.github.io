Lab 08 · Week 8 · October 23, 2026

DC-08 Door Receiver CTF

Pen-test the DC-08 security-door receiver, defeat three successive patches, and capture the flags

Learning Outcomes

By the End of This Lab

  • Recover a device's communication requirements from its output and service documentation
  • Construct and test command packets through a maintenance interface
  • Compare receiver behavior across successive firmware patches
  • Explain why accepted messages do not necessarily establish a trusted sender

The Big Picture

What You Are Doing

  1. You are conducting a penetration test of the DC-08, a receiver for a security door. You have gained access to its USB maintenance interface; your objective is to defeat the lock.
  2. Recover the device documentation, investigate its messages, and capture a flag for each patch you defeat.
  3. A packet workbench is available in your arsenal. Use it to support your investigation; the device determines whether an attempt succeeds.
  4. Complete all three patches. An optional reset challenge is available after the final unlock.

Before Lab

Preparation

  • Review the week's communication and message-checking concepts.
  • Open the Lab 8 report and packet workbench. Use Arduino IDE Serial Monitor on the computer connected to the device.
  • Keep testing within the assigned DC-08 maintenance interface. Do not replace firmware, inspect it for flags, or bypass the interface by driving indicator pins.

Bench Setup

Equipment and Materials

  • Prepared DC-08 receiver and USB data cable
  • Computer with Arduino IDE Serial Monitor and a browser
  • Lab 8 report and packet workbench
  • Disconnect USB before moving wires; use USB power only

Follow These Steps

Lab Walkthrough

1. Gain Access — Table 1

Evidence Photo

Save the recovered schematic address, device identity, and evidence that the device is initialized in the LOCK condition.

  1. You have gained access to the DC-08, a receiver for a security door. Your task is to break into the locked device. A helper page is available in your arsenal.
  2. Connect the device by USB and open Arduino IDE Serial Monitor at 115200 baud, 8-N-1, with a newline at the end of each message. Do not upload another sketch; close other programs using the port.
  3. Secure the device schematic to investigate its addressing, message format, and communication requirements.
  4. To complete this part, initialize the device in the LOCK condition and record your findings in Table 1.

2. Defeat Patch v1.0 — Table 2

Evidence Photo

Save your attempts, the receiver's responses, and the captured patch v1.0 flag.

  1. The maintenance port is leaking a legitimate command. Use the schematic to determine what the message means and what you must send to open the device.
  2. Investigate the command syntax and error-detection method. Record your attempts and the device's responses in Table 2.
  3. Capture the flag once you defeat the lock.

3. Defeat Patch v2.0 — Table 3

Evidence Photo

Save your attempts, the receiver's responses, and the captured patch v2.0 flag.

  1. The device has been patched, and your previous attack no longer works. Find out what changed and break in again.
  2. Record your investigation and attempts in Table 3, and capture the new flag.

4. Defeat Patch v3.0 — Table 4

Evidence Photo

Save the successful attempt, receiver response, and patch v3.0 flag. Include the reset packet, response, and bonus flag if you attempt the extra credit.

  1. The device has been patched again. Find out what changed and defeat the lock once more.
  2. Record your investigation and attempts in Table 4, and capture the final patch flag.
  3. After defeating this patch, congratulations! For optional extra credit, send a valid reset command and capture the bonus flag before starting a new run.

5. Finish and Submit

  1. Complete Tables 1–4, include your evidence and captured flags, and answer the short conclusion. Each student submits an individual report as a PDF.
  2. Close Serial Monitor and disconnect USB before moving wires.

Why This Lab Matters

  • Receiver error checks and sender authentication address different requirements
  • A penetration test must examine what the device actually accepts, not just what a tool calculates
  • Testing is limited to the assigned DC-08 receiver and its maintenance interface
