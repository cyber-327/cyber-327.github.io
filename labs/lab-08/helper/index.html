Packet workbench

Decode the clues. Build a packet. Test your reasoning on the controller.

Preliminary lab tools. This page calculates checks locally; it cannot open a door, award a flag, or confirm what an Arduino accepted. Copy your packet into Serial Monitor. Hex is an encoding, not encryption.

1. Hex ↔ ASCII

Nonprintable bytes are displayed as ·. Their original values stay in the hex input. Decoded text is never executed or opened automatically.

2. Seven-byte message

Enter the 14 hex digits from your capture. Each pair is one byte. Bytes 0–5 are checked; byte 6 stores the check. In stage 1 it must be 00 or 01. Stages 2–3 use CRC-8.

Checked means locked. The check-byte digits start locked. Loading a packet preserves your lock choices.

Write buttons deliberately replace byte 6, even when its search locks are checked. In stage 3, preserve the approved CRC instead.

Enter a packet to inspect.

3. Fixed-CRC collision search

Set the desired command first. Lock the device ID, command, and both target CRC digits. Leave the extra byte adjustable. The search keeps every locked digit unchanged.

CRC-8 parameters: polynomial 07, initial value 00, no reflection, no final XOR. The target is byte 6; the CRC covers bytes 0–5 only. A match means CRC consistency—not authorization or a successful command.

No search started. At most four digits may be unlocked (65,536 candidates).

Send the displayed hex text plus a newline at 115200 baud, 8-N-1. The planned firmware decodes that text into seven bytes. Search runs locally; it does not send trial packets to the board.
