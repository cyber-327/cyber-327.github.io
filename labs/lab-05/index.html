Lab 05 · Week 5 · October 2, 2026

Signal ID CTF: Spectrum, SNR, and Capacity

Fourier/spectral analysis, harmonics, dB, noise, SNR, Shannon capacity, and signal fingerprints

Scheduled duration: 2 hours after Friday lecture

Learning Outcomes

By the End of This Lab

  • Apply the associated communication principles in a controlled investigation: Fourier/spectral analysis, harmonics, dB, noise, SNR, Shannon capacity, and signal fingerprints
  • Collect repeatable measurements and distinguish observation from interpretation
  • Explain how the measured communication behavior affects reliability, visibility, or trust

Before Lab

Preparation

  • Review the Week 5 guide and its equations or signal relationships
  • Read the full procedure and mark every required measurement or capture
  • Create a report document with headings for predictions, results, interpretation, and guardrails
  • Ask the instructor before proceeding if the assigned setup differs from this handout

Bench Setup

Assigned Equipment and Data

  • Computer/spreadsheet
  • Oscilloscope with FFT or spectrum view
  • Function generator

Optional or Specialized

  • instructor-provided captures
  • RTL-SDR for receive-only spectrum viewing

Investigation

Procedure

  1. Record team members, equipment models, probe settings, source settings, and the assigned circuit or capture configuration.
  2. Inspect the setup before power is applied. Confirm grounds, voltage/current limits, terminations, and the safety boundaries listed below.
  3. Predict the baseline result. Include the calculation, expected waveform, expected spectrum, or expected decoded structure appropriate to this lab.
  4. Use a Shannon calculator or spreadsheet to explore bandwidth/noise/capacity tradeoffs
  5. Capture or inspect spectra for sine, square, triangle, AM-like, noisy, and unknown signals
  6. Classify unknown signals by fundamental, harmonics, sidebands, bandwidth, and confidence
  7. Report what can and cannot be inferred from the spectrum
  8. Compare the baseline and changed conditions. Preserve measurements that support the comparison and identify any result that does not match the prediction.
  9. Return sources to a safe state, power down as appropriate, restore the bench, and confirm that all captures contain only approved lab data.

Submission

Required Evidence and Deliverables

  • A labeled setup or block diagram showing signal flow and measurement points
  • Predictions and calculations made before each major measurement
  • A results table with units, instrument settings, and baseline-versus-changed conditions
  • Representative scope, spectrum, logic, simulation, or trace captures with annotations
  • A concise claim-evidence-reasoning explanation of the principal finding
  • A short Signal Intelligence interpretation stating what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain, and which guardrails applied

Completion standard: another technical reader should be able to understand what was tested, reproduce the important settings, and see why the evidence supports the conclusion.

Why It Matters

  • Signal classification is the entry point for lawful SIGINT
  • SNR and capacity quantify reliability under interference

Safety and Scope

  • Receive-only SDR use only
  • No decoding private communications
  • Do not make attribution claims beyond evidence
