CYBER 327: Digital Communications

Draft syllabus: course details may change before final publication. Confirm dates and requirements in class.

Course Description

Every digital message has to travel through the physical world. In CYBER 327, we will build, measure, and investigate the signals that make digital communication possible.

Along the way, we will see how noise, interference, hardware changes, and attackers can affect a communication link. Hands-on labs connect the theory to real measurements, useful tools, and cybersecurity scenarios in a safe, controlled environment.

Prerequisites

  • Calculus
  • First course in physics
  • Basic circuit and programming familiarity are helpful but not assumed beyond course prerequisites

Course Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, you will get to know these topics:

  • Electrical and circuit foundations for communication hardware
  • Signals in the time and frequency domains
  • Noise, bandwidth, SNR, filtering, and channel capacity
  • Analog and digital modulation, sampling, coding, BER, and receiver behavior
  • Wired, wireless, optical, serial, cellular, and satellite communication systems
  • Signal measurement, SDR, interference, spoofing, tamper evidence, and other physical-layer cybersecurity concerns
  • Safe, legal, and evidence-based investigation of communication systems

Materials

  • Required textbook: Electronic Communications
  • Supplementary Chapter 1: DC Circuit Foundations
  • Supplementary Chapter 2: Electronic Measuring Equipment
  • Supplementary Chapter 3: AC Circuits and Components

Labs

Labs are central to the course. They connect principles from lecture to real measurements, signal behavior, and cybersecurity-relevant analysis. The lab is in Room 424 EB, and access is by card swipe. Enrolled students should have card access. Take good care of the equipment and report failures to the TA promptly.

You may work alone or in a group of up to three, but everyone is expected to contribute fairly. Lab attendance is not recorded; however, lab assistants are available only during scheduled lab hours or at other times arranged in advance.

The lab or teaching assistant manages the lab sessions and grades the reports. Availability outside the scheduled lab period will be announced. Talk with the TA or instructor whenever you have questions about the lab, equipment, or homework.

Lab Reports

Lab reports are graded primarily on completeness and technical clarity. Your report should provide a clear, chronological record of what you did, what you measured, and what the evidence means. Record calculations and observations directly in your report rather than on disposable scratch paper.

Polished artwork is not required, but the report must be legible and understandable. The standard is repeatability: another technical reader should be able to understand what you did, how you did it, and why. Include photographs of important setups as evidence of your work.

Submit lab reports through Learning Suite. Unless a lab handout states otherwise, reports are due the day before the next lab at 11:59 PM on Thursday. Even when you work with a group, you are responsible for submitting your own report.

Homework

Homework reinforces lecture concepts through calculation, interpretation, and challenging problems. Show enough work for the grader to follow your reasoning and award partial credit.

Homework is generally due every other Tuesday at 11:59 PM. Submit it through Learning Suite, where it will be graded by a TA.

Late Policy: Extensions and Revisions

This policy applies to homework assignments and lab reports.

Everyone begins the semester with four extension credits. Each credit extends an eligible homework assignment or lab report by 24 hours. You may use no more than two credits on one assignment, and you do not need to provide a reason or request advance permission. Indicate the number of credits used when you submit the work.

After your extension credits are exhausted, you may still submit late work through the last day of class, but the maximum possible grade will be 50%.

You also have two revision credits during the semester. Each credit allows you to revise an eligible homework assignment or lab report and recover up to 100% of the points previously lost on that submission. Revised late work remains capped at 50%. A revision credit does not replace missing work and does not apply to quizzes, exams, or the Excellence Project.

Approved accommodations and exceptional circumstances are handled separately from these credits on a case-by-case basis.

Quizzes

Quizzes are short checks at the beginning of Wednesday's lecture. They normally take 5-8 minutes and typically cover material discussed during the previous week. A quiz may be open-note or open-resource when the questions emphasize application and interpretation rather than recall.

There will be 12 short quizzes throughout the semester. The best 10 scores count equally toward your final grade. This built-in erasure tolerance accommodates two missed or unusually poor quiz results without requiring routine make-ups. A missed quiz receives a zero and is automatically handled by the two dropped scores when available. Additional make-ups require an accommodation or instructor-approved circumstance.

Tests

Midterm tests emphasize applying communication principles and defending conclusions. Dates, time limits, and permitted resources will be announced in class and on the course site.

Excellence Project

The Excellence Project is worth 5% of the course grade and will use a published rubric. Possible directions include:

  • Build or improve a small communication experiment.
  • Analyze a signal or link using lawful, receive-only or lab-owned data.
  • Extend a lab with additional measurements or documentation.
  • Compare media or modulation choices for a realistic secure-link scenario.
  • Create a student-facing study guide or visualization for a difficult communication concept.

Proposal and final submission dates will be announced in class and on the course site.

View the Excellence Project guide, rubric, and example ideas.

Professionalism, Participation, And Attendance

Professionalism includes attendance, preparation, participation, technical care, ethical behavior, teamwork, and respect for lab equipment and classmates. Students are expected to attend class, arrive on time, participate constructively, and complete work even when it is difficult.

Participation matters because communication and security work require clear reasoning, careful questioning, and evidence-based discussion. Questions are encouraged.

Unexcused absences may affect the professionalism grade; excused absences should be communicated to the instructor.

Final Exam

The final examination is worth 15% of the course grade. Its date and time will be announced.

The final assessment will emphasize synthesis: communication-link limitations, media selection, modulation and coding tradeoffs, measurement evidence, cybersecurity applications, and safe/legal boundaries.
