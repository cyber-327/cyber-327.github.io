Lecture 26 / Mon

Media Transmission Case Study: Video, Timing, and Compression

November 2, 2026

Lecture 26 slides (.pptx)

Digital Communications Foundations

  • Follow a live camera through source representation, compression, encryption, link/channel processing, playback buffering and display
  • Pixels, frames, grayscale pixel depth and frame rate; raw video-rate calculation
  • Lossless versus lossy compression, video codec and equal-duration compression ratio
  • Average application throughput versus uneven packet/segment delivery
  • Playback reserve, buffer underflow, latency and arrival jitter
  • Diagnose artifacts with frame, arrival, buffer and encoder evidence

Cybersecurity Connection

  • Reliable bits alone do not establish timely, useful video or authenticated origin
  • Camera availability decisions trade useful detail, required rate and playback delay
  • Encryption protects video content while traffic sizes and timing may remain observable
  • Padding and shaping have throughput or delay costs; playback symptoms do not identify an attacker

Lecture 27 / Wed

Wireless Systems Survey: Wi-Fi, RFID, Bluetooth, IoT, and Cellular

November 4, 2026

Lecture 27 slides (.pptx)

Digital Communications Foundations

  • Bits, modulation, antennas, propagation, filtering and receiver decisions in a shared physical medium
  • Bands and channels used by Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, cellular, RFID, NFC and UWB; illustrative regional frequency examples
  • Why finite spectrum, designated shared bands, standards and practical hardware lead to shared use
  • Overlapping transmissions, relative received power, channel access, retries and useful delivery
  • Real-system connections to OFDM, FHSS, DSSS, channel selection, backoff and managed scheduling
  • Brief MIMO definition for continuity with the following reception lecture

Cybersecurity Connection

  • Deauthentication: a forged management message can disconnect a susceptible established link without decrypting user data
  • Evil twin: an attacker-controlled AP advertises an imitation network; connection depends on settings, checks and user decisions
  • The two attacks may be combined, but neither guarantees the other
  • Match PMF, approved profiles, appropriate identity checks and validated HTTPS to different problems

5G/Cellular Awareness, SDR, and Spectrum Ethics

November 6, 2026

Lecture 28 slides (.pptx)

Digital Communications Foundations

  • Receiver chain, SDR hardware/software and compatible measurement tools
  • Spectrum, waterfall, frequency/time axes, filter width, displayed level and evidence settings
  • Local versus remote AM reception and limits of frequency/audio identity clues
  • Wanted versus unwanted power, SINR and logarithmic conversion using compatible linear measurements

Cybersecurity Connection

Recall and Apply

Definitions and Formulas

Important Definitions

pixel
One picture sample in an image.
frame
One complete video image.
frame rate
Number of video images represented per second.
pixel depth
Number of bits used to represent one pixel before compression.
compression
Representation that reduces required data by removing redundancy or perceptually less important information.
lossless compression
Compression that permits exact reconstruction of the original input data.
lossy compression
Compression that discards information and reconstructs an approximation of the original.
video codec
Paired method used to encode/compress and decode/decompress video.
application throughput
Useful application data delivered per unit time, after the overhead excluded by the stated measurement point.
segment
A short chunk of video data that can be carried in multiple packets.
packet
A unit of data sent through a packet network.
latency
Time between source action and received presentation.
jitter
Variation in arrival timing.
buffer
Stored data used to absorb timing and rate variation.
buffer underflow
The playback buffer runs out of the data needed for presentation.
artifact
A visible or audible distortion that can result from compression, missing data or timing problems.
traffic analysis
Inferring information from communication size, timing or direction without necessarily reading its content.
traffic padding
Adding dummy data to make real traffic patterns harder to distinguish; it consumes capacity.
traffic shaping
Controlling when and at what rate traffic is sent; smoothing can require waiting or extra provisioned capacity.
spectrum
The range of frequencies under discussion.
band
A range of radio frequencies.
channel
A system-defined portion of spectrum and associated transmission rules.
bandwidth
Frequency span, measured in Hz; different from a bit rate.
airtime
Time during which a transmission occupies a shared radio channel.
coexistence
Different radio systems operating in the same environment, potentially sharing spectrum.
interference
Unwanted received signals that can disturb recovery of the wanted signal.
unlicensed band
A band compliant devices can use under stated rules without an individual spectrum license for ordinary use.
Wi-Fi
A family of wireless local-area networking technologies; common modes use OFDM and shared-access rules.
OFDM (orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing)
Organizing parallel subcarriers within a synchronized waveform; not automatic coordination between unrelated networks.
CSMA/CA (carrier-sense multiple access with collision avoidance)
Shared access using channel sensing and protocol backoff; it reduces, but cannot eliminate, collisions.
backoff
Waiting according to a protocol before attempting or retrying channel access.
Bluetooth
A wireless family using 2.4 GHz; connected communication can use adaptive frequency hopping.
FHSS (frequency-hopping spread spectrum)
Coordinated changes of frequency using an agreed sequence and timing.
AFH (adaptive frequency hopping)
Updating shared channel choices to avoid channels found unsuitable.
Zigbee
Low-power device networking; common 2.4 GHz implementations combine DSSS with access and retry rules.
DSSS (direct-sequence spread spectrum)
Representing data with faster code elements and recovering it with matched receiver processing.
chip
One element of a spreading sequence, not a new independent message bit.
cellular
Managed radio access using base stations, scheduling and cells; bands and waveform details vary.
MIMO (multiple-input multiple-output)
Multiple antennas and processing that can exploit spatial paths for reliability or throughput.
RFID (radio-frequency identification)
Reader–tag communication, including LF, HF and UHF implementations.
NFC (near-field communication)
Very short-range interactions using 13.56 MHz near-field coupling.
UWB (ultra-wideband)
Radio occupying a broad frequency span, including GHz-band fine-timing and ranging applications.
ranging
Estimating separation between devices using suitable measurements.
IoT (Internet of Things)
Connected physical devices and services; an application category, not one radio protocol.
beacon
A periodic announcement used to help discover a network.
association
Establishing a connection under a network’s rules; not sufficient identity evidence by itself.
authentication
Checking a claimed identity using the configured security method.
access point
A device providing a wireless connection into a network.
SSID (service set identifier)
The advertised Wi-Fi network name; copying a name does not establish operator identity.
evil twin
An attacker-controlled wireless access point that impersonates a network users expect to trust.
certificate
A signed statement linking an identity to a public key, checked against a trusted issuer and the expected identity.
deauthentication attack
Forging management messages to end a susceptible wireless authentication relationship and disrupt service.
PMF (protected management frames)
Protection for relevant robust management messages after protection is established; not protection against RF jamming.
RF jamming
Intentional radio interference that disrupts reception, distinct from a decoded forged disconnect message.
authentication server
A service checking network login in the selected authentication design.
TLS (Transport Layer Security)
A protocol providing authenticated protection for an application connection when properly configured and validated.
HTTPS
HTTP protected with TLS; proper validation can protect application content over an untrusted network path.
SDR (software-defined radio)
Radio in which important processing occurs in software, with physical receiving hardware still required.
ADC (analog-to-digital converter)
Hardware that converts an analog input into digital samples.
FFT (fast Fourier transform)
A calculation used to obtain frequency components from samples.
waterfall
A frequency-versus-time history whose color represents displayed level.
spectral occupancy
Where and when measurable signal energy is present under stated receiver settings.
noise floor
The displayed background level for the chosen receiver and analysis configuration.
dBFS
Decibels relative to digital full scale; ordinary SDR++ levels are not calibrated dBm.
dBm
An absolute power level relative to 1 mW.
dB
A logarithmic ratio; not a linear power to add in a SINR denominator.
WebSDR
A remotely located software-defined receiver accessed through a browser.
SINR (signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio)
Wanted signal power divided by combined interference and noise power under compatible measurement definitions.
cell
An area served by a base station; actual coverage depends on operating conditions.
base station
Network radio equipment serving user devices in a cellular area.
uplink
Communication from a user device toward the base station.
downlink
Communication from a base station toward a user device.
reference signal
A known signal resource used for measurement or channel estimation.
RSRP (reference signal received power)
Power on specified reference-signal resources, commonly reported in dBm.
RSRQ (reference signal received quality)
A quality ratio relating reference power to total reception and specified measurement bandwidth.
RSSI (received signal strength indicator)
A broader received-power indication with implementation-dependent resource and scaling context.
MIMO (multiple-input multiple-output)
Multiple antennas and processing that can use spatial paths for throughput or reliability.
beamforming
Adjusting antenna-signal amplitudes and phases to favor particular directions.
RF jamming
Intentional radio interference that disrupts reception, distinct from a decoded forged disconnect message.
baseline
A recorded reference condition with enough settings and context to support comparison.
availability
The ability to obtain the required service when it is needed.

Equations and Relationships

  • Raw video ratedata rate = width × height × bits per pixel × frames per second
  • Compression ratioratio = uncompressed size / compressed size
  • Buffer durationtime = stored data / playback data rate
  • Conceptual lectureno new calculation relationships. Distinguish waveform organization, channel access and authentication.
  • SINRSINR = signal power / (interference power + noise power)
  • Power comparisonA 3 dB power change is ≈ a factor of two

Check Your Understanding

Study Questions

  • How do width, height, pixel depth and frame rate set uncompressed video rate?
  • What does a compression ratio compare, and what changes between lossless and lossy compression?
  • Why can a stream fit the average throughput budget yet still freeze or arrive late?
  • Under what conditions does stored data divided by playback rate give a playback reserve?
  • What can encryption hide, and what could an observer still infer from the link?
  • Which measurement would help distinguish encoder settings, arrival gaps and playback waiting?
  • Why can different wireless technologies share the same band yet still interfere?
  • How do Wi-Fi OFDM and shared-access rules solve different problems?
  • How does Bluetooth adaptive hopping connect to FHSS, and how does Zigbee connect to DSSS?
  • Why is a forged disconnect message different from RF jamming?
  • What extra evidence would you require before trusting a familiar network name?
  • A link slows down, another repeatedly disconnects, and a familiar network name appears twice. Which evidence would distinguish interference, forged disconnect messages and an evil twin? Which protection fits each?
  • What would you save with a receiver observation so another investigator can compare it?
  • Why do receiver filter width, observed span and displayed power need different interpretations?
  • Why can local and remote receivers differ at the same tuned frequency?
  • How can wanted power remain strong while quality falls?
  • Which question does each cellular metric answer, and what still requires an application test?
  • What distinguishes RF jamming from Wi-Fi deauthentication?
  • Wanted power is 90 pW, interference is 20 pW and noise is 10 pW. Calculate linear SINR and its dB value. If unwanted activity and message losses increase, what would you test before calling it a jamming attack?
  • Which evidence would distinguish interference from receiver faults or application bottlenecks?
