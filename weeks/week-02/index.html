Recall and Apply

Definitions and Formulas

Important Definitions

parallel circuit
A circuit whose branches share the same two nodes and therefore the same voltage.
branch
One current path between two nodes in a parallel circuit.
equivalent resistance
A single resistance that would draw the same total current as the complete resistor network.
current limit
The maximum current a source is configured or designed to provide before it reduces its output or enters protection.
voltage droop
A decrease in source or rail voltage when load current increases beyond what the source, wiring, or connections can maintain.
loading
A change in circuit behavior caused by connecting another device or measurement instrument.
input resistance
The effective resistance a meter or probe places across the measured points at low frequency.
High-Z
A high-impedance instrument setting or input that draws relatively little current from the measured source.
reference or ground
The point against which voltage is measured; a scope ground clip is a real electrical connection to this point.
probe attenuation
The ratio by which an oscilloscope probe reduces a signal before it reaches the scope input, commonly 1x or 10x.
amplitude
The maximum change of a waveform from its center or reference level.
peak-to-peak voltage
The voltage difference between a waveform's maximum and minimum values.
period
Time required for one complete cycle of a repeating signal.
frequency
Number of cycles completed per second.
RMS
The effective value of an AC signal that produces the same average power in a resistor as an equivalent DC value.
DC offset
The average or center voltage of a waveform relative to its reference.
DC coupling
Oscilloscope input mode that displays both the changing signal and its DC offset.
AC coupling
Oscilloscope input mode that blocks the DC component and can attenuate low-frequency content.
trigger
Oscilloscope condition that establishes a stable horizontal starting point for acquisition.
accuracy
Closeness of a measurement to the true value under stated conditions.
precision
Repeatability or spread among repeated measurements.
resolution
Smallest increment an instrument can display or distinguish.
