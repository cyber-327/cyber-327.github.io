Recall and Apply

Definitions and Formulas

Important Definitions

phase
The angular position of a periodic waveform relative to a stated reference waveform at the same frequency.
phasor
A magnitude-and-angle representation of a sinusoidal quantity at one frequency; this course writes phasors in polar form using RMS magnitude.
inductance
A conductor's tendency to oppose changes in current by storing energy in a magnetic field.
inductor
A component designed to provide inductance and store energy in a magnetic field.
inductive reactance
The frequency-dependent opposition an ideal inductor presents to alternating current.
transformer
Coupled coils that transfer AC energy through a changing magnetic field.
turns ratio
Ratio of secondary turns to primary turns that sets the ideal transformer voltage ratio.
galvanic isolation
Separation that prevents a direct conductive path between circuits.
saturation
Core condition in which additional magnetizing force produces little additional magnetic flux and the ideal model breaks down.
self-resonance
Frequency where an inductor's inductance and parasitic capacitance resonate, limiting ideal inductive behavior.
coupling
Intended or unintended transfer of energy between circuits or conductors.
EMI
Electromagnetic interference that degrades or alters circuit operation.
common mode
Signal component appearing in the same direction on multiple conductors relative to a reference.
near field
Region close to a source where electric or magnetic coupling can dominate.
containment
Engineering controls that reduce unwanted signal transfer or exposure.

Equations and Relationships

  • Sine-wave modelv(t) = Vpeak sin(2 π f t + phi)
  • Sine-wave RMSVrms = Vpeak / √(2)
  • Ideal inductor voltagevL(t) = L di(t) / dt
  • Inductive reactanceXL = 2 π f L
  • Ideal inductor current magnitudeIrms = Vrms / XL
  • Ideal inductor phasevoltage leads current by 90 degrees; current lags voltage by 90 degrees
  • Ideal transformer voltage ratioVs / Vp = Ns / Np
  • Ideal transformer current ratioIs / Ip = Np / Ns
  • Ideal impedance reflectionZp = (Np / Ns)2 Zs
  • Coupling amplitude ratioH = Vreceived / Vsource
  • Measured reductionreduction percent = (Vbefore - Vafter) / Vbefore × 100%
Practice focus: Identify amplitude, frequency, and phase in a sinusoidal model; represent a same-frequency sinusoid as an RMS phasor; calculate inductive reactance and ideal RMS current; and explain why inductor voltage leads current. Calculate ideal transformer voltage and current ratios, then identify which conclusions require checking frequency, loading, isolation rating, or other real-coil limitations. Explain how frequency, distance, orientation, twisting, routing, shielding, and ferrites affect measured coupling, and distinguish a measured interference path from an unsupported claim about intent.
