Recall and Apply

Definitions and Formulas

Important Definitions

network medium
Physical path carrying communication signals.
physical layer
Signaling, timing and medium behavior.
data-link layer
Local frame organization, delivery fields and error checks.
single-ended reception
Measuring a signal relative to a reference or return.
differential reception
Measuring the voltage difference between two input conductors.
common-mode pickup
A similar disturbance on both conductors relative to a reference.
crosstalk
Unwanted coupling from a neighboring signal path.
regeneration
Deciding received symbols and retransmitting fresh levels and timing. An incorrect decision can be retransmitted cleanly.
CAN
Controller Area Network, a two-wire differential bus connecting controllers.
transmission line
A path whose distributed electrical properties and propagation must be considered when modeling the signal.
rise time
Time for a transition to move between specified low/high fractions of its final change. Compare its timescale with path delay, not only the bit interval.
propagation delay
Travel time for a signal feature along a stated path.
bit duration
Time allocated to one bit. A long bit duration can still include a short edge rise time.
wavelength
Distance between corresponding points on successive cycles of a repeating wave, such as adjacent peaks at one instant.
electrical length
Physical length expressed relative to wavelength at the frequency of interest.
characteristic impedance
Voltage/current ratio of a single traveling wave on a uniform line. It is distinct from a conductor's DC resistance.
termination
The electrical arrangement presented at an interface; suitable matching controls reflections.
reflection
A wave returning after an impedance change.
attenuation
Reduction of signal strength along a path; calibrated insertion loss measures delivered-power reduction in the specified setup and can include mismatch effects.
skin effect
Increasing concentration of alternating current near conductor surfaces as frequency rises, increasing effective conductor resistance.

Equations and Relationships

  • Differential voltageVdiff = VA - VB
  • Equal pickup in the ideal model(VA + n) - (VB + n) = VA - VB
  • Unequal pickup leaves error(VA + nA) - (VB + nB) = (VA - VB) + (nA - nB)
  • Real noise rejection is finite and frequency dependent; observe receiver input limits.
  • One-way delaytd = length / propagation velocity. Use the specified or measured cable velocity.
  • Wavelengthλ = v/f, with v in m/s and f in Hz, giving λ in metres. The vacuum speed c is ≈ 3.00 × 108 m/s; a cable typically has a lower propagation velocity.
  • An approximate screening guideline is length ≥ λ/10. It is not a sharp failure boundary, maximum bit rate or guarantee that shorter paths have negligible resistance/loading. For digital edges compare propagation delay with rise time and account for relevant high-frequency content.
  • Worked examples assume a separate cable with v = 0.64c = 1.92 × 10^8 m/s. A 0.25 m length equals λ/10 when λ = 2.50 m and f = 76.8 MHz. At 60 Hz, λ = 3200 km and λ/10 = 320 km. This velocity is not a universal power-line value or an Ethernet specification.
  • Traveling-wave impedanceZ0 = Vwave / Iwave. Total V/I where reflected waves overlap need not equal Z0.
  • Positive link losslossdB = inputdBm - outputdBm.
  • Cascaded compatible losses under the stated test assumptions add in dB; subtract total loss from the input dBm level to predict output.
  • Geometry and dielectric material affect propagation and impedance. More length of the same uniform cable adds delay and usually loss, rather than simply multiplying its nominal Z0.
  • Skin effect has no universal 10 MHz onset. Frequency-dependent loss and reflections can reduce receiver margin; actual errors and achievable rate depend on the complete link.

Study Questions

  • How does a receiver use two input voltages?
  • Start with V_A = 1.0 V and V_B = 3.0 V. Add 0.6 V to both, then another 0.3 V to A only. Calculate the difference after each step. What else is needed to predict a bit decision?
  • Why does twisting support differential noise rejection?
  • How would you test for crosstalk while keeping the comparison controlled?
  • How do geometry, interfaces and physical access affect media choice?
  • Why do finite noise rejection and message authentication address different problems?
  • When does a simple connected-wire model omit important propagation behavior?
  • What distinguishes propagation delay, edge rise time and bit duration? Why can a slow bit pattern still require transmission-line analysis?
  • Explain λ = v/f and the approximate λ/10 guideline. Why do 25 cm at 76.8 MHz and 320 km at 60 Hz both reach it when v = 0.64c?
  • Why is a cable's nominal characteristic impedance different from a continuity-meter resistance?
  • How can the same path introduce delay, loss and reflections?
  • A 24.0 m path has v = 2.00 × 10^8 m/s. Find one-way delay. A calibrated test applies −4.0 dBm and delivers −11.0 dBm; find loss, then predict output with another 3.0 dB.
  • What does a repeatable before/inserted/restored comparison establish, and what further evidence would a tampering claim need?
