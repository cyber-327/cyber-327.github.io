Excellence Project

Choose one focused way to investigate, demonstrate, build, teach, or improve something in Digital Communications.

What It Is

The Excellence Project is a small, self-directed way to explore, demonstrate, or improve something connected to Digital Communications. It is worth 5% of the course grade. The goal is not to build a large capstone project; it is to do one focused thing well and show clear evidence of what you learned.

Plan for approximately 3-5 hours of work per student. You may work individually or in a team of up to three students with instructor approval. Team submissions must identify each member's contribution.

Choose A Path

  • Measure or demonstrate: Create a repeatable experiment that makes a communication concept visible or audible.
  • Build or improve: Build a small circuit, instrument setup, or physical demonstration, or improve an existing course lab.
  • Create a course resource: Produce a concise visualization, guide, video, or interactive aid that helps future students understand a difficult concept.
  • Investigate: Study a communication technology beyond normal class depth and explain its physical-layer operation and security relevance.
  • Teach or serve: Help others learn a course-related concept and document both the activity and its technical substance.

Project Ideas

These prompts are adapted from projects and requests from previous versions of the course. You may propose one of these or bring your own idea.

  • Compare sine, square, triangle, voice, or musical signals in the time and frequency domains.
  • Demonstrate AM or FM signals in the time and frequency domains, including how noise changes the result.
  • Create an aliasing demonstration using audio sampled at several rates.
  • Build a visual demonstration of harmonics produced by different instruments playing the same note.
  • Demonstrate parity, a checksum, or a CRC with an Arduino or another small hardware-based link.
  • Compare link behavior as distance, orientation, attenuation, interference, or filtering changes in an authorized lab setup.
  • Create a one-page or short-video explainer on UWB, spread spectrum, the 5G physical layer, radio astronomy, or hiding information in noise.
  • Analyze an instructor-provided, lab-owned, or lawful receive-only signal recording and explain what its spectrum reveals.
  • Improve a lab procedure, diagram, troubleshooting guide, or study resource and test it with another student.
  • Develop another focused project that connects communication signals, systems, measurement, and security.

Proposal

Submit a short proposal before beginning. It should state:

  • The question, concept, or problem you will address.
  • What you will make, measure, investigate, or teach.
  • The evidence you will submit.
  • The equipment, software, signals, or data you will use.
  • Any teammates and each person's planned role.
  • Any safety, transmission, privacy, or authorization concerns.

Wait for instructor approval before purchasing equipment, transmitting signals, collecting data involving other people, or testing anything outside the isolated lab environment.

What To Submit

  • The project artifact or evidence, such as measurements, plots, photographs, a short video, circuit files, code, or a student-facing resource.
  • A concise written explanation, approximately one page, describing what you did, what the evidence shows, what you learned, and why the work demonstrates excellence.
  • For teams, a brief contribution statement from each member.
  • Enough setup detail that the instructor can understand or reproduce the result.

A small project with convincing evidence is stronger than an ambitious project that cannot be completed or explained.

Safety And Authorization

  • Use only instructor-approved frequencies, power levels, equipment, accounts, networks, signals, and datasets.
  • Do not intercept private communications, test systems without permission, disrupt services, or transmit outside the approved lab setup.
  • Jamming or interference experiments require explicit instructor supervision and an isolated or conducted setup.
  • Follow normal electrical, RF, equipment, privacy, and laboratory safety rules.

Dates

Proposal and final submission dates will be announced in class and on the course site. Early proposals are encouraged so equipment and scope can be checked before work begins.
